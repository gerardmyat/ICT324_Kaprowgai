Core Research Question (1 Sentence)
“To what extent can supervised machine learning and association rule mining accurately predict whether a first-season television anime receives a verified sequel within three years, based on public audience engagement metrics and production metadata?”
Section 01

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

In the contemporary Japanese animation industry, a substantial proportion of television anime series conclude after their initial broadcast season without receiving a sequel, even when they garner favorable critical reception or sizable global audiences. Decisions regarding franchise continuation are typically governed by multi-firm production committees (seisaku iinkai) composed of publishing houses, television networks, merchandise licensors, and streaming distributors.

The Core Problem

Although production committees evaluate commercial viability, source material longevity, and studio scheduling capacity, internal financial deliberations remain proprietary. Consequently, external industry stakeholders—including international streaming platforms, regional licensing agencies, and media analysts—lack an empirical, reproducible framework for estimating the likelihood of long-term franchise continuation.

Recent data mining scholarship utilizing publicly accessible MyAnimeList (MAL) repositories has predominantly focused on three analytical tasks: predicting aggregate user rating scores prior to broadcast using multimodal deep learning (Armenta-Segura & Sidorov, 2024, 2025), forecasting temporal genre popularity trends via time-series modeling (Setiawan et al., 2023), and constructing unsupervised clustering architectures for content recommendation (B. S. Pratama et al., 2025; Putra et al., 2024).

The Literature Gap: Predicting whether a first-season television anime secures a subsequent season represents a distinct structural problem because critical popularity and commercial continuation frequently diverge. A critically acclaimed original series may terminate after a single season due to exhausted narrative material, whereas a moderately rated adaptation with resilient viewer retention may receive multiple sequels. To advance beyond existing studies on public anime datasets, this research formulates sequel production within a three-year post-broadcast window as a supervised classification problem and integrates Association Rule Mining (ARM; S. F. Pratama & Priyanto, 2024) to identify interpretable multi-attribute co-occurrence patterns.

Section 02

2. Research Questions

To address both predictive performance and managerial interpretability, the study is guided by three formal research questions:

RQ1 • Predictive Classification

Sequel Prediction Accuracy

To what extent can supervised classification algorithms accurately predict whether a first-season television anime receives a verified sequel or continuation film within three years based on public metadata and engineered audience engagement metrics?

RQ2 • Feature Attribution

Influential Determinants

Which institutional, content, and behavioral attributes—such as audience size, rating volume, viewer completion-to-drop ratio, source material, animation studio, genre, episode count, and producer count—exert the strongest predictive influence on sequel production?

RQ3 • Association Rule Mining

Multi-Attribute Configurations

Which frequent multi-attribute configurations (antecedents) exhibit the strongest statistical association with sequel continuation (has_sequel = 1) versus single-season termination (has_sequel = 0)?

Methodological Cohort Selection & Bias Mitigation

  • Right-Censoring Prevention: The sampling frame is restricted to television series (Type = TV) whose initial seasons premiered between January 2010 and December 2022. Concluding in 2022 guarantees a minimum three-year longitudinal observation period (extending through 2025–2026) for every title.
  • Prequel Filtering: Entries linked to an existing prequel relation are systematically excluded to ensure only true first seasons enter the cohort.
  • Target Variable Definition (has_sequel): Coded as 1 if a qualifying television sequel or canonical continuation film was released or verifiably announced within three years of the first season's completion date, and 0 otherwise (excluding non-canonical OVAs, recaps, and promotional specials).

4. Feature Domains & Engineering Protocols

In addition to standard catalog attributes, the study introduces domain-specific engineered variables to capture viewer commitment and retention dynamics:

Feature Domain Operational Variables Transformation & Engineering Protocol
Audience Engagement Member count, Favorites count, Completed count, Dropped count Log-transform right-skewed counts; derive Engagement Intensity Ratio (Favorites / Members) and Viewer Retention-to-Drop Ratio (Completed / Dropped).
Audience Ratings Mean score (1–10 scale), Scored-by user volume Median imputation with missingness indicator; z-score standardization for linear models.
Production Structure Animation studio, Listed producer count, Licensor presence Consolidate top 15 studios + 'Other' category; treat producer count as institutional proxy.
Content Attributes Source material (Manga, Light Novel, Original, etc.), Genres, Episodes, Age Rating Multi-hot binary encoding for genres; one-hot encoding for source material and age rating; Source-Normalized Episode Pacing.
Broadcast Context Premiere season (Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall), Premiere year (2010–2022) Categorical seasonal encoding; temporal cohort control for industry streaming expansion.
Section 05

5. Planned Methodology: Six-Phase CRISP-DM Pipeline

The research strictly adheres to the Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining (CRISP-DM; Schröer et al., 2021), supported by a formal Data Diary documenting all transformations:

Phase 1

Business & Domain Understanding

Frame three-year sequel renewal as a quantitative decision-support task for content acquisition and licensing stakeholders, establishing empirical benchmarks that surpass univariate popularity heuristics.

Phase 2

Data Understanding & EDA

Perform exploratory data analysis across the 2010–2022 cohort to quantify target class imbalance, inspect distributional skewness, and visualize empirical sequel rates across source materials, studios, and genres.

Phase 3

Data Preparation & Discretization

Execute entity resolution between Kaggle records and Jikan API relational graphs, remove prequel-linked seasons, apply log/categorical encodings, and discretize continuous features into quantile tiers (Low, Medium, High) for transaction-based ARM.

Phase 4

Modeling: Classification & ARM Track

Supervised track: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost. Unsupervised pattern-discovery track: Apriori and FP-Growth algorithms constrained to consequents {has_sequel = 1} and {has_sequel = 0}.

Phase 5

Evaluation & Model Explainability

Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation evaluated via macro/minority F1-score, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC. SMOTE and cost-sensitive weighting inside training folds to prevent leakage. Global/local interpretability via SHAP; ARM filtered by Support, Confidence, and Lift (> 1.20).

Phase 6

Deployment & Scholarly Reporting

Synthesize empirical findings into a conference-standard research manuscript, archive the documented codebase and curated dataset in the project repository, and formulate actionable licensing insights.

Computational Environment & Tooling

Python 3 pandas NumPy scikit-learn imbalanced-learn XGBoost mlxtend (Apriori & FP-Growth) SHAP Matplotlib / Seaborn Jikan REST API v4
Section 06

6. Expected Contributions & Limitations

Expected Academic & Practical Contributions

  • Curated Longitudinal Cohort: A clean, verified dataset of 2,000–3,000 first-season TV anime (2010–2022) accompanied by a fully reproducible Data Diary.
  • Comparative Rigor: Evaluation of four machine learning classifiers under class-imbalance protocols paired with high-lift association rules.
  • Actionable Industry Heuristics: Interpretable decision-support evidence demonstrating how source material, studio reputation, and viewer retention interact to drive franchise longevity.

Methodological Limitations

  • Unobserved Revenue Streams: Lack of accessible data for physical media (Blu-ray/DVD) sales, overseas streaming licensing fees, and merchandise sales.
  • Announcement Timeline Jitter: Production cycles can occasionally delay sequel announcements slightly beyond the strict 3-year threshold.
  • Observational Association: Findings describe statistical association and predictive correlations rather than direct financial causality.
