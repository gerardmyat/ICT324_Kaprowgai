Research Focus Chapter 01 // Core Inquiry & Sub-Questions

Research Questions

"To what extent can supervised machine learning and association rule mining accurately predict whether a first-season television anime receives a verified sequel within three years, based on public audience engagement metrics and production metadata?"
🎯

RQ1: Predictive Classification

To what extent can supervised classification algorithms accurately predict whether a first-season television anime receives a verified sequel or continuation movie within three years using public metadata and engineered engagement ratios?

📊

RQ2: Feature Attribution

Which institutional, content, and behavioral attributes — such as audience size, rating volume, viewer completion-to-drop ratio, source material, studio, genre, episode count, and producer count — exert the strongest influence on sequel production?

🔗

RQ3: Association Rule Mining

Which frequent multi-attribute configurations (antecedents) exhibit the strongest statistical association with sequel continuation (has_sequel = 1) versus single-season termination (has_sequel = 0)?

Industry Problem Chapter 02 // The Committee Black Box

The Production Committee Problem

In Japan's animation industry, sequel greenlights are decided behind closed doors by Production Committees (seisaku iinkai) — consortia of manga and light novel publishers, television networks, music record labels, toy manufacturers, and international streaming distributors.

🔒

Private Financial Data

Outsiders cannot view physical Blu-ray sales profit margins, domestic broadcast timeslot ratings, merchandise licensing agreements, or overseas minimum guarantee contracts.

📉

Research Blind Spot

Past anime research focuses primarily on user recommendation systems, rating predictions, and seasonal popularity trends — leaving sequel continuation completely unaddressed as a formal classification task.

⚡

Acclaim vs. Continuation

Critical acclaim and commercial continuation frequently diverge: a critically acclaimed 9/10 original anime may end forever, while a 6.8/10 harem comedy manga adaptation receives four consecutive seasons.

Research Process Chapter 04 // CRISP-DM Methodology

CRISP-DM Research Architecture

We strictly adopt the Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining (CRISP-DM) six-phase methodology to structure the inquiry:

PHASE 01 // BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING

Business Understanding

Formalizing sequel continuation as an objective binary classification target: has_sequel = 1 if a verified continuation airs within 36 months of Season 1 premiere, else 0.

PHASE 02 // DATA UNDERSTANDING

Data Understanding

Profiling 24,000+ Kaggle records. Identifying that sequels cannot be inferred from flat files alone, prompting integration with Jikan REST API v4 for relational sequel/prequel link graph harvesting.

PHASE 03 // DATA PREPARATION

Leakage-Safe Data Prep

Filtering TV first-season cohort (2010–2022, N = 2,450). Log-transforms, median imputation, Z-score scaling, and SMOTE / class-weighting are placed strictly inside each CV training fold to prevent data leakage.

PHASE 04 // MODELING TRACK

Modeling & Baselines

Comparing Majority-Class Baseline against Logistic Regression and Random Forest (initial baselines), Decision Tree, and XGBoost, alongside Apriori and FP-Growth association rule mining.

PHASE 05 // EVALUATION & METRICS

10-Fold Stratified CV

10-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation (fixed seed = 1992) with nested inner CV for hyperparameter tuning. Reporting Accuracy (± std), Precision, Recall, F1-Score, ROC-AUC, and Confusion Matrix.

PHASE 06 // DEPLOYMENT & EXPLAINABILITY

Deployment & SHAP

Extracting SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to provide global and local feature explainability, demonstrating how individual attributes influence the model's decisions.

Data Engineering Chapter 05 // Variables & Transformations

Key Mining Variables & Transformations

Raw metadata often misleads machine learning algorithms due to power-law distributions. We engineered domain-specific ratios to capture genuine audience commitment:

Domain Raw Metadata Variables Engineering & Transformation Rationale
👥 Engagement Member Count, Favorites, Completed, Dropped Log-transform applied: log10(members). Derived Fan Loyalty Ratio (Favorites ÷ Members) and Viewer Retention Ratio (Completed ÷ Dropped) to normalize for raw popularity bias.
⭐ Ratings Mean Score (1.00 – 10.00), Scored-By Count Median imputation with binary missingness indicators. Evaluated as non-linear splines since high ratings alone do not guarantee sequel commercial viability.
🏢 Production Studio Name, Producer Count, Licensor Presence Top 15 studios categorized into distinct capacity tiers (Tier S / Tier A / Other). Producer count engineered as proxy for institutional committee backing and sponsor risk distribution.
📖 Content DNA Source Material (Manga, LN, Original), Genres, Episodes, Age Rating Multi-hot vectorization for 20+ genre tags; one-hot encoding for source material. Episode counts binned into standard broadcast cours (1-cour vs 2-cour).
📅 Broadcast Cohort Premiere Season & Year (2010 – 2022) Categorical season encoding (Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter). Temporal cohort boundaries ensure 3-year post-broadcast observation window maturity.

🛡️

Zero Data Leakage (In-Fold Prep)

Every preparation step — median imputation, log10 outlier transform, StandardScaler Z-normalization, and SMOTE oversampling — is encapsulated inside an imblearn.pipeline.Pipeline fitted strictly on the 9 training folds before scoring the held-out test fold.

🔬

Nested Tuning & Noise Check

Hyperparameters were selected via inner 3-fold grid search on the training split (never tuned on test folds). Ensemble trees beat linear/single-tree models by +9% to +14% F1 (exceeding fold noise), while Random Forest (81.6%) and XGBoost (83.1%) are within 1 standard deviation (±2.5%) and thus statistically comparable.

🧮

Out-of-Fold Confusion Matrix (XGBoost)

Across N = 2,450 out-of-fold predictions: TP = 482 (sequels caught), FN = 126 (missed sequels), FP = 281 (false alarms), and TN = 1,561 (correctly identified 1-season shows), yielding 79.2% Recall and 72.7% Precision.

Milestone #3 Diagnostic

What Is Not Working Yet, Methodological Limitations & Ethical Scope

While our ensemble models substantially outperform baseline classifiers, three technical bottlenecks remain under active refinement: the model still produces false positives (FP = 281) on viral one-shot promotional adaptations (such as No Game No Life) that achieve massive member counts without continuation; static 2023 MyAnimeList snapshots risk retroactive popularity inflation after sequels air (currently being addressed via ratio-feature ablation); and initial association rules were dominated by ubiquitous genres before imposing stricter Lift constraints (≥ 1.50). Furthermore, because private production committee financials (e.g., Blu-ray sales, broadcast fees, and merchandise royalties) are inaccessible and public community scores carry Western demographic bias, our findings establish observational correlations rather than causal renewal rules. Consequently, this system is designed strictly as an exploratory decision-support screening tool for analysts and researchers, and it must never be used to automate greenlight or cancellation decisions, evaluate creator or studio workforce performance, or substitute for comprehensive financial due diligence.

4. Outliers & Skewness Members: 30k – 2.5M+ Engagement counts exhibit extreme power-law right skewness (skewness > 4.2). Applied log10(x + 1) transformation and engineered bounded ratios (Favorites ÷ Members, Completed ÷ Dropped) capped at the 99th percentile inside CV folds.
