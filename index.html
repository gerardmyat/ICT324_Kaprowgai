Will It Get a Season 2?

Predicting Television Anime Sequel Production Using Classification & Association Rule Mining

Myat Htoo Kaung — 6708426 Aung Kabar — 6708217 Aphisith Vongxay — 6708522

Rangsit University International College • Academic Year 2026

Research Question

"To what extent can supervised machine learning and association rule mining accurately predict whether a first-season television anime receives a verified sequel within three years, based on public audience engagement metrics and production metadata?"

The Problem

Most television anime end after one season — even popular ones. Sequel decisions are made by private production committees (seisaku iinkai) using internal financial data that outsiders cannot access. Streaming platforms, licensors, and analysts currently have no data-driven way to estimate whether a show will continue.

Existing research on anime data focuses on predicting ratings, genre trends, and recommendations — but nobody has tackled sequel prediction as a classification problem. Critical acclaim and commercial continuation often diverge: a beloved original may end in one season while a mid-rated adaptation gets three sequels.

Our Approach

We frame sequel production as a binary classification task: did the anime receive a sequel within 3 years? (has_sequel = 1 or 0). Our study combines:

  • Classification: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost.
  • Association Rule Mining: Apriori and FP-Growth to discover interpretable patterns linked to sequel production.
  • Explainability: SHAP values for feature importance and model transparency.

We follow the CRISP-DM six-phase methodology, with stratified 5-fold cross-validation and SMOTE for class imbalance.

Data Source

Our primary dataset contains 24,000+ anime records, filtered down to a cohort of approximately 2,000–3,000 first-season TV anime premiering between 2010 and 2022 — well above the course requirement of ≥ 500 real rows.
